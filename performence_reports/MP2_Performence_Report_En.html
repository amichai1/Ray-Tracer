Mini-Project 2 – BVH Acceleration Performance Measurements

Rendering time report for the required acceleration and multithreading configurations
Amichai Mukades & Michael Dabah  ·  ISE5786  ·  2025–2026

This report documents the measured rendering times for a fixed scene of 510 geometries across 14 test configurations, covering all combinations of flat / manual / automatic BVH structure, CBR on/off, and three threading modes (single-thread, raw Java threads, and parallel IntStream). A dedicated four-configuration benchmark was run on a guaranteed-identical scene to produce the mandatory comparison. Mini-Project 1 (3×3 anti-aliasing, 9 rays per pixel) was active in all configurations. All render times are shown in seconds.

Scene specification

Geometries: 510 total
Resolution: 600 × 600 pixels
Spheres: 500 in a 20 × 25 grid (r = 8, spacing = 20)
Anti-aliasing (MP1): 3 × 3 = 9 rays / pixel
Other bounded: 2 accent spheres, 2 cylinders, 3 triangles
Reflections: mirror sphere KR = 0.85
Unbounded: 2 infinite planes (ground + back wall)
Transparency: glass sphere KT = 0.85
Light sources: 2 Directional, 2 Point, 1 Spot (5 total)
Effects: shadow, reflection, transparency

Required configuration summary

Baseline: mp2_B1_noAccel_noMT = 157.692 s (mandatory benchmark, same scene for all four configurations). "Acceleration on" = automatic BVH + CBR enabled. Both threading methods (raw threads and parallel stream) are shown for each acceleration state.

Key observations

  • BVH + CBR delivers a 14.22× speedup in the mandatory benchmark (single thread, same scene), confirming the algorithm's effectiveness on 510 geometries at 600×600 with 9-ray anti-aliasing.
  • Best overall: 21.41× (Auto BVH + CBR + parallel stream). Stream mode consistently outperforms raw threads, likely due to ForkJoinPool's work-stealing scheduler providing better load balance than the synchronized PixelManager used by raw threads.
  • Raw threads show no benefit without BVH (0.98×). With anti-aliasing active (9 rays/pixel), memory bandwidth becomes the bottleneck. The PixelManager's synchronized nextPixel() call creates contention, and the additional thread-management overhead negates any parallel gain.
  • CBR on a flat list degrades performance (0.34×). The AABB slab-method test requires six divisions per geometry. When most of the 500 spheres fall within the camera's field of view, few rays are rejected early, so the AABB overhead is incurred for every sphere without reducing the intersection count.
  • Auto BVH without CBR is the slowest configuration (370.4 s). Building a balanced binary tree of depth ≈ log₂(500) ≈ 9 introduces deep recursion and extra Geometries-node traversal. Without CBR the AABB of each node is never checked, so the hierarchy adds overhead without providing any culling benefit.
  • CBR is only beneficial when paired with a hierarchy. Manual + CBR gives 11.30× over flat + CBR; Auto BVH + CBR gives 29.58× over flat + CBR. The hierarchy is what turns the O(n) per-ray traversal into O(log n).

Fastest recorded configuration: mp2_14_cbr_auto_stream at 7.365 seconds (21.41× vs. baseline).
Mandatory benchmark best: mp2_B3_bvh_noMT at 11.086 seconds (14.22× vs. baseline).
